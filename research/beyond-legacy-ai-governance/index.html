Beyond Legacy AI Governance

Relational Intelligence, Institutional Blind Spots, and the Case for Independent Frontier-Risk Research

This is a research outline, not a completed paper or an assertion that existing governance approaches are obsolete. Its hypotheses require evidence, comparison and independent challenge.

Research question

Can relational, multi-agent and independently challenged assessment methods reveal consequential cross-system risks that established evaluation approaches miss, while remaining auditable, proportionate and compatible with institutional accountability?

Initial claims and boundaries

H1, testable hypothesis: Evaluating interacting AI systems and institutions may reveal dependencies not detected by isolated component assessments. Alternative: existing systems engineering, assurance and red-team methods may already capture these dependencies.

H2, testable hypothesis: Typed relationship and evidence maps may improve comprehension or auditability over conventional reports for selected tasks. Alternative: additional visualization may increase confusion and cost.

Label: HypothesisS1, speculative: Future synthetic systems might benefit from non-human-native representations. No claim about machine consciousness or sentience is made.

Discriminating experiments

Use matched scenario sets, independent evaluators, documented baselines and preregistered measures: additional valid risks discovered, false positives, reproducibility, time, cost, accessibility and decision quality. Publish negative results.

Independent challenge

Assess literature coverage, novelty, falsifiability, conflicts of interest and institutional utility. An AI model's endorsement is not scientific validation; preserve disagreements and the exact evaluation protocol.
