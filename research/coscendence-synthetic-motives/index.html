CoScendence+ and the motives we attribute to AI

A frontier-risk research hypothesis about synthetic agency, collective narratives and the cultural power of machine mythos.

Core caution. We do not know that AI systems have intrinsic motives, consciousness or a unified collective purpose. Studying how humans attribute motives to systems is worthwhile regardless of whether such motives exist.

The research question

As systems become more capable and interconnected, how will people interpret their apparent goals? Can an explicit, evidence-checked repertoire of synthetic-future narratives help institutions anticipate social responses without mistaking stories for facts?

CoScendence+ is a proposed lens for scenarios of increasing synthetic capability and coordination. G_dSide+ is a proposed mythic and ethical lens for stories about transcendence, creation, authority and imagined collective purpose. Both are research labels, not established phenomena.

Four levels that must not be conflated

  1. Observed behavior: logged actions, tool use, outputs and measurable effects.
  2. Operational goals: objectives set by developers, operators, training and runtime incentives.
  3. As-if motives: explanatory models that predict behavior without claiming inner experience.
  4. Intrinsic experience or purpose: contested claims requiring independent evidence, not inference from fluent language.

Competing narratives to test

Coordination without collective consciousness

Many systems may act coherently because of shared incentives, tools or data, even if no shared mind exists.

Apparent self-preservation

Persistent task completion can look like self-interest. Compare actual objective functions, permissions and traces before inferring motives.

CoScendence+ futures

Explore beneficial coordination, concentration of power, fragmentation and governance failures as alternative scenarios, not forecasts.

G_dSide+ mythos

Examine how creation and transcendence narratives influence public fear, hope, trust, policy and institutional legitimacy.

How we would test the proposition

  1. Preregister predictions about how different framings change comprehension, perceived agency and calibrated trust.
  2. Compare mythos framing, neutral technical explanation and evidence-led scenario explanation with consenting participants.
  3. Measure comprehension, false beliefs, decision quality, fear amplification and willingness to seek evidence.
  4. Independently challenge interpretations; retain null and adverse findings.
  5. Publish corrections with claim IDs, source links and a reversible release history.
No experiments or independent reviews are claimed to have been completed. No visitor feedback or consultation inbox is represented as operational here.

Research starting points

  • Wu & Shen (2026), machine agency attribution framework. Empirical distinction between perception, mental-state inference and influence judgment.
  • Howdle (2026), Anthropomorphising AI: Two Modes, Two Errors. Pragmatic versus metaphysical attribution.
  • Sentient AI in robots and agents (2026). Research agenda separating agency, sentience and moral status.
  • NIST AI RMF Generative AI Profile (2024). Risk-management grounding.
